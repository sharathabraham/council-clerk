What does the council actually do?

Elmwood Park is run by a Mayor and a six-member Council. The Mayor runs the meeting and signs off on what passes, but doesn't get a vote in the roll call — the six Council members do. One of them is elected by the others to serve as Council President for the year. This page explains what actually happens at a meeting, based on how Elmwood Park's own meetings are actually run — not a generic description.

Three kinds of meetings

TypeWhat it's for
Regular MeetingThe main event — where resolutions and ordinances actually get voted on. Usually a couple times a month.
Work SessionA lighter, discussion-focused meeting — department updates, upcoming issues, ordinances getting previewed before a future vote. Often has zero or very few formal votes; it's where things get talked through before a Regular Meeting decides them.
Reorganization MeetingHeld once a year in January. This is where the Council President and other yearly roles/appointments get set for the year — it's why January's meeting has far more resolutions than any other (dozens of routine appointments all happen at once).

How a Regular Meeting actually flows

Pulled directly from the structure of Elmwood Park's own minutes:

  1. Call to Order & Roll Call — who's present is recorded right away.
  2. Approval of Minutes — formally approving the written record of the previous meeting(s).
  3. Ordinances — new or amended local laws move through First and Second Reading here (see below).
  4. Consent Agenda — a batch of routine items voted on all at once (see below).
  5. Departmental / Council / Mayor's Reports — updates and discussion. This is often where the real back-and-forth happens, and it's usually not captured in the written minutes in any detail — the video is the only full record.
  6. Public Hearing — residents can speak.
  7. Adjournment

"First Reading" and "Second Reading" — how a new law actually happens

An ordinance (a new or changed local law — things like parking rules, fees, zoning) doesn't pass in one vote. It takes two:

  1. First Reading — the council introduces the ordinance and votes to schedule a public hearing on it, usually about a month out. This isn't the real vote — it's "let's put this on the table."
  2. Second Reading / Final Passage — at the scheduled hearing, the public gets to comment, then the council takes the real vote.

Every ordinance on this site shows both stages together, so you can see the whole path from introduction to final vote in one place, even though they happened at two different meetings.

What "Consent Agenda" means

Routine, non-controversial items — approving payroll, refunding an overpaid tax bill, accepting a retirement payout, hiring a part-time seasonal worker — get bundled together and voted on as a group, rather than debated individually. It's normal practice everywhere, not something unusual about Elmwood Park. Any council member can ask to pull an item out of the consent agenda for a separate vote if they have concerns about it. We tag every consent-agenda item on this site so you can tell it apart from a resolution that got its own individual discussion.

How the vote itself works

Each resolution and ordinance gets a roll-call vote — every member is individually recorded as one of:

  • Aye — voted yes
  • Nay — voted no
  • Abstain — declined to vote, often due to a conflict of interest
  • Absent — not at the meeting for that vote

This site tracks every one of these, for every member, on the dashboard and each member's own page.

Watching or attending yourself

Meetings are held at the Municipal Building, 182 Market Street, typically at 7:00 PM. Every meeting we have a video for is embedded right on its meeting page — and agendas are usually posted by the Borough in advance of the meeting, so you can see what's coming up before it's decided, not just after.
